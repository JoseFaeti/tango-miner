# False-positive analysis: suspended Anki candidates

Date: 2026-10-26

## Project and documentation policy

The project mines Japanese from material the learner intends to read. Its
priority is **high recall**: keep useful vocabulary, grammar, fixed
expressions, colloquialisms, and uncertain candidates rather than risk losing
learning material. “False positive” in this document means a candidate whose
target or definition is demonstrably misleading; it does not mean that every
non-lexical or multi-token expression should be excluded.

Suspicious candidates are review signals and regression-test seeds, not a
blacklist or an automatic removal queue. Future pipeline changes must preserve
useful candidates by default and use confidence/provenance to improve their
target form, examples, or definitions.

Documentation must not contain absolute local paths or other personal machine
details. Refer to workspace-relative paths or describe supplied files
generically.

## No-user-dictionary baseline (2026-10-30)

The baseline is now Sudachi's full system dictionary with `userDict` empty;
JMdict continues to supply compound matches and definitions. The former user
dictionary was generated by looking up sentence substrings in JMdict and using
the same Sudachi parameters for each entry. A match alone does not establish a
word boundary or meaning in context, and those entries may distort analysis.
The comparison below motivates the change but does not prove either tokenizer
is universally correct.

The 10:31 and 10:53 completed archives used the same input manifest and resolved
pipeline configuration; their archived patches show the user dictionary enabled
in the former and disabled in the latter. Both used the same recorded system
Sudachi dictionary and JMdict. The no-user-dictionary run had 17,976 valid and
2,346 review/dropped candidates versus 18,044 and 2,325 previously. Across
both statuses, 232 candidates appeared, 279 disappeared, 28 changed status and
3,972 shared candidates changed frequency. Candidate changes are not measures
of correctness or Anki changes. The earlier Anki plan was applied; the newer
plan was cancelled.

| Observation | Status and next action |
| --- | --- |
| `おてんば` and `かもしれん` appeared as valid; `いざという時` moved to valid | Useful observed gains; preserve source-supported occurrences in future regressions. |
| `うさんくさい`, `ありえない`, and `あっち` disappeared; `かと思えば` moved from valid to review | Recall/selection changes to investigate, not proof that the words no longer occur. Trace source occurrences and pipeline decisions. |
| `あっちい` appeared with a “hot” definition and example `なのにあっちいけって` | Likely misleading example for `あっち行け`; inspect occurrences and preserve genuine `あっちい` uses. |
| `今日は` increased from 344 to 2,021, with greeting definitions but examples meaning “today” + は | Existing target/definition mismatch became more prominent; distinguish the greeting and topic-marked date with source evidence. |
| `だが` decreased from 2,198 to 78; `〜んだ` increased from 26,516 to 37,859 | Investigate tokenization, grammar, merging and counting before judging correctness or designing fixes. |

Occurrence evidence now retains every eligible occurrence of a lemma within a
sentence, rather than only the last one. Example selection and fallback choose
an occurrence whose components support the selected definition and highlight
that occurrence. This fixes a general evidence-loss case; it does not yet
separate meanings into cards, change frequencies or resolve the examples in the
table. Competing merge analyses and contextual classification remain future work.
The no-user-dictionary test suite also exposes older analyzer-dependent
expectations that need reassessment; failures do not by themselves establish
whether the new tokenization or the old expectation is correct.

Historical issues and fixes below were assessed with earlier configurations.
They remain evidence and regression seeds, not a current defect count. Recheck
each relevant case with the no-user-dictionary baseline; an absent candidate
may reflect lost recall rather than a fix. No historical issue has yet been
verified as unnecessary solely by this comparison. Full corpus comparisons
require an agreed corpus and Anki disabled or output-only.

## System-dictionary regression follow-up

The configured system dictionary can split 時には into 時 + に + は;
the occasional ことがある rule now recognizes this contiguous frequency
cue. A targeted merge repair recovers 悪い and こと before continuative し
when Sudachi treats 悪いこと as a proper noun, and another recovers ちげぇ
from the observed それ + はち + げ + ぇ analysis. Both retain original token
components. Other tokenizations and contexts remain unclassified by these
repairs; standalone proper-noun filtering remains an open recall gap.

Sentence-initial んだ can be an interjection even when Sudachi labels it as
nominalizer plus copula. Its component evidence is now distinguished from
explanatory occurrences so it cannot supply examples for that definition.
The system dictionary also splits concessive ものの into nominal もの + の;
that ambiguous analysis remains lexical rather than being reserved as grammar.
These targeted regressions are not a corpus-wide precision or recall estimate.

## Updated dictionary boundary follow-up

The newer dictionary has lexical entries for past-looking expressions such as
`来た` and `できた`. When Sudachi supplies a verb stem and past auxiliary,
the merger now retains the verb rather than selecting an interjection in a
non-isolated predicate. Isolated interjections and entries with a prenominal
sense before a noun remain eligible. This is a conservative heuristic: a
prenominal past clause and a lexical prenominal expression can still be
ambiguous, and standalone interjections can also have verbal readings. A
case-marked argument, preceding verbal noun, or following grammatical noun can
now retain the verb in examples such as `理解できた気がする`. For `できた`
and `出来た`, definition selection uses source-supported `人` prenominal or
standalone exclamation evidence; unclassified occurrences stay available for
review and do not supply examples for those senses. The `人` cue is still
ambiguous when a person is created or obtained. Utterance-initial `参ったな、...`
can remain an interjection, while a relative or quoted past predicate remains
`参る`. The particle exception is limited to `参る`/`まいる`; other conversational
interjections and noun contexts remain incompletely classified.

The merger also preserves the case particle boundary in `からは` and the
comparative `ときより` immediately after a past relative clause, while retaining
causal `からは` and adverbial `ときより`. These focused examples do not establish
corpus-wide precision or recall. Expectation `はず` now selects the JMdict
auxiliary sense when a source-aligned predicate and following copula or sentence
ending support it; example selection uses only supported occurrences. Nominal
homophones without that cue remain unresolved, and the heuristic can still
confuse a noun modified by a predicate. The standalone proper-noun filtering
gap remains separate.

## Vocalizations and sampled boundary follow-up

The output excludes source-evidenced repeated laughter and panting vocalizations
such as `ははは`, `ぐふふ`, and `はぁはぁ` at frequency filtering. It retains
ordinary words, descriptive onomatopoeia, and ambiguous analyses; other sound
spellings are not comprehensively covered. The meaningful scolding `こら`
remains a candidate. Other standalone proper nouns are still filtered, except
for the narrowly repaired `りせ` name context below.

Source-aligned `です` + `きっと` is recovered in the observed contexts where
Sudachi reads `すきっと`; genuine adverbial `すきっと` remains eligible. The
observed `りせ` followed by name-compatible particles, copula, or `ちゃん`
no longer becomes the unrelated verb `りする`; genuine `利する` and its negative
inflection remain eligible. Original token evidence is
retained. These boundary cues are not comprehensive. The lost `招き` and
`ほくほく` examples still tokenize as useful candidates in focused checks;
their archive disappearance may involve frequency or source coverage and
requires occurrence-level evidence before claiming recovery.
